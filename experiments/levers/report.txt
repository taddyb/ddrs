BOTTOM LINE
- The runoff multiplier is not a lever. The model's volume is unbiased on the training years (pred/obs 1.004 at the
  median gauge, seed 42; 1.001 seed 43); the +4.5 % test-period excess is a Q' drift that 1982-1995 cannot see. Honest
  multipliers move the median by +0.0005 to +0.0008; the earlier +0.005 for a 0.95 scalar was chosen on test years.
- Head variance is real but small at the median: the seed gap (0.005) is gamma wandering; fixing gamma = 0 is worth
  about +0.002 vs seed 42 (+0.004 vs the two-seed mean); a two-seed ensemble adds only +0.001 over its members' mean.
  Training is near a skill plateau: epoch 40 -> 50 adds +0.0013 (interval includes 0) on fixed test years.
- The lever that moves the median is a multi-product Q' ensemble: route the working recipe on UH, dHBV2-distributed and
  the daily CudaLSTM Q' and average with weights fitted on the training years: 0.7650, +0.0259 [+0.0221,+0.0309],
  75 % of gauges up, KGE +0.0043. Member choice was test-free (forward selection on training-year summed Q').
- Together: ensemble +0.026, gamma = 0 (and optionally learned p, q) +0.001 to +0.005 inside the ensemble, dams +0.003
  gives +0.029 to +0.033. +0.03 is reachable only with the ensemble; without it the honest non-dam levers sum to
  <= +0.007 and, with dams, reach about +0.01 at most.

4. WHAT TO IMPLEMENT FIRST
First: multi-product Q' forcing. Cheapest form (no engine change): train the working recipe on UH, DIST and daily-LSTM
(already done for seed 42) and average the routed test outputs with the training-year weights (0.50 / 0.25 / 0.25).
Engine form: let data_sources.streamflow take a weighted list of stores and average Q' per divide and day at read time
(average only over stores that cover the divide: the LSTM store has 288,421 divides vs 197,088, and absent divides are
filled with 0.001 today, which would dilute the mean), then train one routing head jointly on the blended Q'. Expected
+0.02 to +0.026 median NSE, KGE about +0.004. Risks: routing is mildly nonlinear in Q (depth, gamma), so input blending
is not identical to output averaging and must be measured with one training run against the output ensemble; part of
the test gain is the members' opposite volume drifts cancelling in 1996-2010 (the training years support the weights,
but the cancellation may not hold in other periods); smoother hydrographs (alpha 0.87) mean KGE barely moves; three
stores must exist for any new domain or period (not for the global runs today); each store's head learns a different n,
so an output ensemble has no single parameter field for the paper, whereas the input blend keeps one head.
Second, together with the first: fix gamma = 0 (learnable_parameters: [n], config only). About +0.002 and it removes the
seed-to-seed drift; a narrow gamma box ([0, 0.1]) or a prior toward 0 is the alternative if gamma must stay. Learning p
and q as well adds about +0.004 more (single seed) but conflicts with the fixed-channel design chosen for
identifiability (q is flat at 85 % of well-fit gauges), so that is the user's call.
Do not implement a learned per-reach runoff multiplier: trained on 1982-1995 it will learn about 1 (the honest global
scalar is worth +0.0005) and, being free per reach, it would absorb timing and routing errors (the review's warning) with
no test-period benefit to show for it. Compare any future volume term against the plain scalar null (+0.0005) and the
ensemble, not against 0.95. Longer training or a flatter lr schedule alone: about +0.001 per 10 epochs on seed 42 and
riding on gamma's drift, so fix gamma first and only then judge whether more epochs pay.
