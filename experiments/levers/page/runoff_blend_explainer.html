Multi-Product Runoff Forcing

Two experiments are running. One builds a new way to feed runoff into the routing: a weighted blend of three runoff products instead of one, the only lever we found that can lift the population median NSE by about +0.03. The other measures how well the gauges can pin down each dam parameter. This page explains both.

Background

What runoff forcing is

ddrs does not make runoff. For each of the 346,321 MERIT unit catchments it takes the runoff that drains into that reach, called Q′ (lateral inflow, m³/s), from a hydrologic model run beforehand, and routes it down the river network with Muskingum-Cunge. Routing can delay and smooth that water, and the dam law can store and release it, but whatever is wrong in Q′ reaches the gauge. Today every run uses one Q′ product: dHBV2 with a unit hydrograph (UH).

Why it helps. This is the standard multi-model ensemble gain (the ambiguity decomposition of Krogh and Vedelsby, 1995), not a routing result. At every gauge, exactly: blend NSE = weighted mean of the members' NSEs + how much the members disagree (their spread around the blend, divided by the variance of observed flow). The weaker members cost 0.026 against UH; their disagreement removes 0.051 of squared error; net +0.019 per gauge, +0.026 at the population median. 76 % of that disagreement is at the event scale (under 30 days), 16 % seasonal, and only 6 % year-to-year volume. So the products mostly miss different storms, and averaging cancels those misses. The LSTM helps because its errors correlate only 0.62 with UH's, not because it is good (alone it scores 0.636). Weighted, 75 % of gauges improve.

How the members and weights were chosen, without looking at the test years. Starting from UH, products were added one at a time while they improved the training-year (1986 to 1995) summed-runoff score: DIST, then LSTM, then no further product helped (dHBV2 lumped and HydroDL were rejected). The weights 0.50 / 0.25 / 0.25 were fitted on routed training-year runs (1986 to 1995). The test years played no part in either choice.

What the engine build changes. The offline estimate averaged three separately routed runs. The engine version averages the runoff first and trains one routing head on the blend, so there is still one set of channel parameters. Routing is mildly nonlinear, so these two can differ; the new runs measure that.

Where it stands against the target

Every lever against +0.03

Hypothesis

If the population median is limited by runoff errors that no routing or dam law can fix, only a lever that changes the runoff itself can move the median by about +0.03, while the dam and routing levers stay in the thousandths.

Plain English

Each row is one change and the median NSE change it brings over all 2,365 gauges. Rows marked measured come from full training runs; the others are offline estimates, fitted on training years and scored on test years.

Result. The dam recipe moves the population median by about +0.004 (two seeds, +0.009 and −0.002). A runoff multiplier fitted honestly gives +0.0005, and fixing γ at 0 about +0.002. The blend gives +0.026 on its own. Inside the blend, γ = 0 adds about +0.001 with p and q fixed (as in the running experiment), and about +0.005 if p and q are learned too. Conclusion. The blend is the only change that gets near +0.03. With p and q fixed it needs the dam recipe's few thousandths on top to get there. The running experiment will show whether the engine version, one head trained on the blended runoff, matches these offline estimates.

Before relying on it

What to watch for

KGE barely moves. The blend smooths the hydrographs (variability ratio 0.914 to 0.869), which NSE rewards and KGE penalises. The weighted blend lifts the median KGE by +0.004 (interval includes 0) and leaves it unchanged gauge by gauge; the equal-weight blend loses 0.005 gauge by gauge.

A little of the gain may be period-specific. The blend gains +0.018 on the training years and +0.026 on the test years. In 1996 to 2010, DIST runs high and the LSTM low on volume, but year-to-year volume differences make up only 6 % of the members' disagreement, so most of the gain is not drift cancelling.

All three stores must exist for any new domain or period, which rules out the global runs today.

The model changes meaning. One routing head trained on a blend of three products is not the same as a head trained on one product. For the paper's inflow-product comparisons, the single-product runs remain the reference.

One seed each so far for the offline estimates; the two no-dam seeds differ by 0.005 at the median, which the new two-seed runs will account for.
