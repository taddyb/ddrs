Why the pool cannot be learned from off (section 5): at z = 0.05 d the pool is full on 97-99 % of flood days, so kc
and phi never enter the forward and get no gradient; z's own gradient is proportional to the volume the pool moves
(slope 1 in z), so on the shelf it is 7 % of its halfway value and its sign agrees in only 69 % of training years; at
32 updates per pooled dam and per_dam_lr 0.02 even a perfectly consistent sign could only reach 0.65 d; and a per-dam
L2 of 1e-3 (summed over dams, against a data loss averaged over ~250 gauges) balances the init gradient at
r_z = 0.002, i.e. z x 1.01. The engine agrees on every point it can check. Decoded checkpoints: z stayed at a median
0.050 d at the 69 after 200 steps without L2 (none above 5 d) and at 0.0499 d (max 0.054 d) under L2 1e-3. Fixed-table
replays (section 6) show the shelf itself in the hourly engine: a 0.05-d pool is indistinguishable from no pool
(+0.0002 test NSE, at most 0.0011 at any gauge), a 1-d pool recovers 13 % of the fitted pool's test gain, 5 d 60 %,
and 20 d all of it, matching the offline harness at every z (the pool's own gain within 0.0006 NSE at the median,
per-gauge Spearman 0.92-0.98).

1. INSTRUMENT (landscapes.py, lawgrad.c, summarise.py, classify.py, transfer.py)
- Harness: experiments/reservoir/laws_v6/v6.py + laws.c (reproduces the rulecurve_offline L2/L4 fits to 4e-14).
  Laws: L4 = bucket T0 + harmonic rule curve (fits_by_gauge.csv), L2 = plain bucket, FA = flood pool + bucket
  (laws_by_gauge.csv). 214 on-reach dam gauges (T0, amplitude); the 69 on-reach flood-control dams of
  experiments/reservoir/smoke/fixed_FA.csv (pool; the same 69 as the engine replays).
- Objective: the engine's training loss nse-batch, L = mean (Q - O)^2 / (sigma + 0.1)^2 on the training days, sigma =
  the gauge's observed std over WY1982-1995. L is 1 - NSE_train up to sigma^2/(sigma+0.1)^2 (median 0.99), so loss
  and NSE units coincide, as in the routing census.
- Coordinates = the engine's raw coordinates (src/nn/dam_params.rs): ln T0 (delta = ln T0/T0_head, head init
  4.5 h), rule-curve amplitude s (fitted coefficients x s, init s = 0), ln z (r_z ~ ln z / 4 below 30 d, init
  0.05 d), ln kc (init 3), logit phi (init 0.5). Curvature H = d2L/dx2 at the 1-D training optimum (quadratic fit
  over +-0.2 units; three-point FD agrees). Bands: contiguous range around the optimum within 0.02 / 0.005 of the best
  training (or test) NSE, in decades for T0, z, kc. Flat = a factor-2 move costs < 1 % of L*, the routing census rule
  (research/why-analysis/A-flat-q.md), evaluated at the optimum. Multimodal = >= 2 local minima of prominence 0.002.
- Gradients at the engine init and halfway (raw-coordinate midpoint) to the optimum: 1-D for T0 and amplitude; for
  the pool JOINT (T0 0.1875 d, kc 3, phi 0.5, z 0.05 d all at once, as the engine starts) with a pathwise gradient
  (lawgrad.c, forward-mode through the branch each min/max takes, i.e. what autodiff sees; it matches tiny-step FD
  of laws.c exactly away from kinks, check_pgrad.py).
- Transfer: test NSE at the training optimum vs the test optimum, the gain from init to optimum on train vs test
  years, and split-half stability (optima on WY1983-89 vs WY1990-95).
- 2-D planes (41 x 41) at the 69: (log T0, log z) and (log z, log kc).
- Routing on the same scale: the 5-year all-gauge census (landscape-p21-all-5yr, 2,124 well-fit gauges), H_nn and
  H_qq at the optimum rebuilt from the stored eigenpairs.

3. PER PARAMETER
T0, per-dam residence time (fig 1 col 1, fig 2).
- The 1-D landscape in ln T0 is a shelf and a wall: below ~0.3-0.5 d the daily bucket is pass-through and the loss
  is flat (the daily objective cannot see sub-daily storage, the same mechanism as routing n below one day of travel
  time); above the optimum the loss rises steeply. Whether T0 is identifiable therefore depends on where T0* sits.
- T0* < 0.5 d (42 % of on-reach dams): flat at 80 %, band open to the pass-through limit. Learning T0 there learns
  nothing and costs nothing. T0* 0.5-2 d (21 %): stiff, 0.02 band about one decade (this is the "band spans a decade"
  of the indirect evidence). T0* > 2 d (36 %, mostly DOR > 0.5 and flood-control): the stiffest group, H 0.10, 0.02
  band 0.5 decades, stiffer than the median routing n; but the head's 4.5 h init sits on the shelf, so half of them
  start with a gradient under 25 % of the steepest gradient on the way.
- Transfer: 78 % of the training gain survives on test years; the test-year optimum is 0.2 decades from the training
  one; using the other half's optimum costs 0.005 NSE (split halves disagree beyond half the 0.005 band at 58 %).
- Verdict: learnable from the gauge as is where T0* is 0.5-2 d; learnable only with a good init where T0* is days
  (start at ~1-2 d or from storage / inflow, not 4.5 h); not identifiable below 0.5 d, where it can be left at the
  head or fixed small.
- Side finding (checkpoint decode, read_dams_mpk.py): under per_dam_l2 1e-3 the per-dam delta never moved (S4 run
  2026-09-27T23-36-04Z: |delta| median 0.005, p90 0.07; rule-curve theta median 0.016, i.e. c ~ 0.008), as the L2
  balance below predicts (equilibrium |delta| ~ |dL/d delta| / (2 lambda G) ~ 0.02). The per-dam terms of that run
  stayed near zero (offline fits have |c| median 0.054), so most of its +0.0168 must have come from the shared release
  head (inferred from the decode, not tested by an ablation). Without the L2 (2026-09-30T02-46-23Z)
  delta moved (median |delta| 0.30) and the trained T0 tracks the offline FA T0 (Spearman 0.68 at the 69).

Rule-curve amplitude (fig 1 col 2).
- Curved at the optimum (H 0.041 in ln s, as curved as routing n), flat at only 19 %, no plateau (the landscape is a
  near-quadratic bowl in s, so the gradient at s = 0 is twice the halfway gradient), no multimodality.
- But the training optimum does not hold: the gain from s = 0 to the training optimum is +0.019 on training years and
  +0.0036 on test years (ratio 0.19; 30 % of dams lose on test years); split halves of the training period disagree
  by 0.43 in s. The gauge identifies the training years' seasonal operation precisely, and that operation changes
  from year to year (the 30-120 d band is operator decisions, laws_v6 section 1).
- Verdict: learnable from the gauge as is, but not worth learning: what it identifies is not stationary. Drop it,
  or keep it only behind shrinkage strong enough to cut its training gain to the +0.004 it keeps out of sample.
  (Also note the engine's rule_curve_max 0.5 caps coefficients that the offline fits put above 0.5 at 11 % of dams.)

Pool size z (fig 1 col 3, fig 3, fig 5).
- Sigmoid in ln z: a lower shelf (z < ~1 d, the pool fills in the first hours of every flood and moves almost no
  volume), a rise between ~1 and ~20 d, and an upper plateau where the pool never fills and z has exactly zero
  gradient. The optimum (median 17 d offline) sits on the knee.
- At the optimum: curved (H 0.045, same as routing n), flat at 17 %, 0.005 band 0.73 decades; the fitted pool is
  outside the 0.005 band of the init at 86 % of dams, so the gauge can tell a real pool from no pool.
- Transfer: the best of all dam parameters (test gain 1.12x the training gain, test optimum within 0.23 decades,
  ratio 1.06), though split halves still disagree (54 %; a pool's value rests on the few largest floods of each half).
- From the init: the joint pathwise gradient at the engine init is 7 % [3 %, 12 %] of the halfway gradient and 1.7 %
  of the steepest gradient on the path; 72 % plateau-at-init.
- In the engine the same shape holds on test years (section 6): opening the pool from 0.05 d to 1 d gains +0.004
  (13 % of the fitted pool's gain), to 5 d +0.015 (60 %), to 20 d +0.027 (all of it).
- Verdict: learnable only with a good init. Started at 5-20 d it stays and transfers; started at 0.05 d it does not
  move.

Release target kc (fig 1 col 4).
- The stiffest dam parameter at its optimum: H 0.54, ten times routing n; 0.005 band 0.07 decades (17 %).
- Rugged: 2-3 local minima at 97 % of dams, because each value of Qc = kc Ibar admits or excludes discrete flood events.
  The test optimum is close (factor 1.03) but the split-half optima differ beyond the band at 71 %, and the training
  optimum costs 0.006 test NSE against the test optimum (the largest of the five).
- From a shut pool kc gets 8 % of its halfway gradient (it enters only on the 1-3 % of flood days where the 0.05-d
  pool is not full). Once the pool is open its gradient at kc = 3 is healthy (path ratio 0.71).
- Verdict: learnable only with a good init: an open pool and kc near the right basin. Treat as per-dam calibration
  (grid search, as the offline fits did) or tie it to a physical quantity (spillway or downstream channel capacity
  over mean inflow), not gradient descent from 3.

Capture share phi (fig 1 col 5).
- No interior optimum at 67 % of dams: the training loss wants phi = 1 (capture everything above the target); the
  0.005 band spans phi 0.65 to 1 at the median. Fixing phi = 1 costs 0.0000 test NSE at the median (p75 0.0006).
- At the engine init its gradient is dead (3.7e-6 in logit units, 3 % of halfway) and points to SMALLER phi at 65 %
  of dams (with a nonzero gradient); the engine-trained phi did fall (0.50 to 0.467; direction agrees with the
  offline init gradient at 64 %).
- Verdict: not identifiable (the optimum is the boundary phi = 1 and the gauge cannot separate 0.65 from 1), and not
  needed. Fix phi = 1 and drop the parameter.

4. DAM AND ROUTING PARAMETERS ON ONE SCALE (fig 2)
Ordered by curvature at the optimum, in ln units of the parameter, nse-batch loss, same flatness rule:
  kc 0.54 (flat 4 %)  >  T0 with T0* > 2 d 0.10 (1 %)  >  routing n 0.047 (28 %)  ~  pool z 0.045 (17 %)
  ~  rule-curve amplitude 0.041 (19 %)  >  T0 overall 0.023 (40 %)  >  routing q 0.0004 (80 %)  ~  T0 with
  T0* < 0.5 d 0.0001 (80 %)  ~  phi (boundary).
So curvature at the optimum alone would call every dam parameter except phi and short T0 "at least as identifiable
as roughness". The two extra axes of the instrument are what separate them: the pool parameters fail on
reachability (plateau at init), the rule curve fails on stationarity, phi fails on having no interior optimum. The
routing analogue: n passes all three above one day of travel time; q fails curvature and stationarity (its optimum
flips sign between windows). The paper's rule then reads the same for both families: learn a parameter only if it
is curved at the gauge's optimum, reachable from the init, and stationary across windows.

5. WHY THE POOL CANNOT BE LEARNED FROM OFF (fig 3, fig 5)
(a) kc and phi are absent from the forward. At z = 0.05 d (72 minutes of mean inflow) capture is capacity-limited on
    97 % of training flood days (99 % at the fitted kc, phi) and evacuation is pool-limited on 90-93 % of
    evacuation days: c = Fmax - F and e = F, neither involves kc or phi. Pathwise |dL/dr_phi| at the init is 3.7e-6
    (halfway 1.1e-4); |dL/dr_kc| 9e-4 (halfway 0.011).
(b) z's own gradient is proportional to the volume it moves. dL/d ln z rises with slope ~1 in z from 0.05 to ~3 d
    (fig 3b), so at the init it is 7 % of the halfway value. Over the shelf the training NSE rises by 0.0035 from
    z = 0.05 to 1 d at the median dam (under 0.005 at 64 % of dams), against 0.010 to 5 d and 0.017 to 20 d. The
    hourly engine shows the same shelf on test years: +0.0042 to 1 d (under 0.005 at 58 % of gauges), +0.0145 to
    5 d, +0.0272 to 20 d, against offline test +0.0041, +0.0142, +0.0267 (section 6).
(c) Its sign is noisy. At the init, the 13-year gradient wants a larger pool at 77 % of dams, but per water year only
    69 % of years agree (median). Adam moves a parameter by about lr x (consistency), so the drift is a fraction
    of per_dam_lr.
(d) The step budget is short. In the S6pool run (2026-09-30T02-46-23Z, per_dam_lr 0.02, l2 0, 200 steps) a pooled dam
    received 32 updates on average (7,505 dam-updates over 233 pooled dams), so even a perfectly consistent sign
    caps z at 0.05 e^(4 x 0.02 x 32) = 0.65 d; reaching 17 d needs r_z = 1.50, at least 75 consistent updates.
    Observed: at the 69 dams the trained z has median 0.050 d (IQR 0.019-0.175), 6 % above 1 d, none above 5 d,
    against the offline median of 17 d; how far z moved tracks the per-year sign consistency (Spearman 0.64,
    fig 5b), and its direction agrees with the offline init gradient at 71 % of dams.
(e) The per-dam L2 holds it shut. The engine adds lambda sum_d |r_d|^2 once per step to a data loss that is a mean
    over ~250 gauges' days, so one dam's data gradient is |dL_gauge/dr| / 250. Balancing gives r_eq =
    |dL/dr| / (2 lambda G): at lambda = 1e-3 and the median init gradient, r_z = 0.0024 (z x 1.01). Decoded from the
    L2 = 1e-3 S6pool checkpoint (2026-09-30T01-53-15Z, epoch 13): z median 0.0499 d, max 0.054 d at the 69. For the
    L2 to let z reach 17 d, lambda must be below ~2e-6 (init gradient) to ~3e-5 (halfway gradient), fig 5c. At the
    optimum the data curvature in r_z per step (16 x 0.045 / 250 = 0.003) is only comparable to 2 lambda = 0.002, so
    1e-3 is also a strong prior there; at the init the data curvature is ~zero or negative (the shelf bends down into
    the rise), which is what "more curvature at the init than the data" meant.
Net: the plateau is not a gradient bug. It is the forward model: a pool that is full on every flood passes no
information about kc or phi and moves too little volume for z to matter, so the loss is a shelf until z reaches
about a day. The engine replays confirm the shelf is in the engine's forward too, not an artefact of the daily
offline harness.

surface that barely moves.

7. WHAT DDRS SHOULD DO WITH EACH
- T0: learn per dam (keep per_dam_t0), with per_dam_l2 0 or <= 1e-5, initialised near the right decade (1-2 d, or a
  storage/inflow prior) instead of the head's 4.5 h; accept that below 0.5 d it is unidentified and harmless.
- Rule curve: drop (or shrink hard). It is identifiable on training years and not stationary; +0.004 out of sample
  for 4 coefficients per dam.
- Flood pool z: do not learn from off. Initialise it open (5-20 d, the offline median 17 d, or NID flood storage /
  Ibar) and let it learn from there with no per-dam L2 toward 0.05 d, or calibrate it per gauged flood-control dam.
  Do not initialise at the engine's 120-d cap either: a pool larger than the largest flood never fills and z has
  zero gradient there (10 of the 69 offline fits sit above 120 d, up to 3,650 d, on that upper plateau).
- kc: calibrate per gauged flood-control dam (grid) or prescribe from a physical capacity; if learned, only with the
  pool already open and kc started in the right basin.
- phi: fix at 1 (drop).
- Ungauged dams: none of this applies; every verdict above is per gauge (the coefficients were not predictable from
  NID features, RF R2 <= 0.15, earlier work).

8. CAVEATS
- Offline and daily: the engine is hourly and its capture uses the previous step's inflow; the offline sits on the
  routed gauge series (the dam reach is double-counted). The engine replays reproduce the offline pool landscape at
  every z tested (pool's own gain within 0.0006 NSE, Spearman across gauges 0.92-0.98), so the z shelf is not a daily-step
  artefact. The T0 shelf below 0.5 d was not replayed in the engine and is partly a daily-step property.
- The engine check is of the forward (test-year skill along z with the other parameters fixed), not of the engine's
  gradient along z; the gradient side rests on the offline pathwise gradient plus the trained-checkpoint decode
  (z did not move, direction agreement 71 %, Spearman 0.64 with per-year sign consistency).
- 1-D slices hold the other parameters at their JOINT fit, so slice gains are conditional (the FA T0 was co-fitted
  with the pool; with the pool shut it is no longer optimal, which is why the offline z = 0.05 sits -0.008 below L2
  and the engine z = 0.05 replay -0.005 below the twin).
- G = 250 gauges per step is from the S6 runs (n = 37,740 valid gauge-days per step at 150 scored days); the L2 balance
  scales with it.
- The routing numbers are from a different window (WY1996-2000, test years) and a different model (p = 21 census);
  the comparison is of curvature per ln unit in loss units that are NSE units in both.
- Split-half stability is noisy for the pool: a 6-7 year half has few big floods.
