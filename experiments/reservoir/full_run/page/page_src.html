What this run shows, and where the evidence is

{{CLAIMS}}
The test

What was compared, and on which years

Training · WY1982-1995 · 14 years
Test · WY1996-2010 · 15 years · scored once

Three predictions at every gauge

  • Summed Q′: the dHBV2 runoff of every catchment upstream, summed each day. No routing, no learned parameters: the floor any router must clear.
  • No dam: Muskingum-Cunge routing over {{REACHES}} MERIT reaches, hourly, with a KAN head that maps catchment attributes to roughness n₀ and its stage exponent γ, n(d) = n₀ (d / 1 m)^−γ.
  • Dam release: the same model, plus a storage bucket at each of the {{N_DAMS}} dam reaches.

The split

  • Both arms trained on 1981-10-01 to 1995-09-30 in 90-day windows: 50 epochs, 500 Adam updates, NSE loss, seed {{SEED}}, CPU. Identical configuration except the dams.
  • Scored on {{WINDOW}} ({{N_DAYS}} days, water years 1996-2010) with the final checkpoint. No early stopping, no model selection on the test years.
  • The same {{N_ALL}} gauges appear in both periods. This is a hold-out in time, not in space.

The release law

  • Each dam reach stores S = T(t)·Q and releases Q, with T(t) = T₀ exp(a sin ω + b cos ω), ω = 2π · day of year / 365.25, T at least 1 hour. Storage is conserved.
  • T₀ (1 hour to 365 days), a and b (±2) come from a second KAN head fed 19 NID features of the dam: storage, surface, drainage area, height, year, purposes. Trained jointly with the routing head.
  • No dam operations data enter. The only information about any dam's behaviour is the gauge record downstream, used as the loss target in the training years.

Gauge groups

  • Dammed ({{N_DAMMED}}): at least one NID dam of 10 MCM or more upstream. Undammed ({{N_UNDAMMED}}): none that large (smaller dams can exist).
  • Dam on the gauge reach ({{N_ONREACH}}): a large dam sits on the MERIT reach that holds the gauge.
  • Degree of regulation: upstream NID storage divided by a year's mean flow volume at the gauge. 1 means the reservoirs could hold a full year of flow.

Routing beats summed Q′, with or without dams

Hypothesis

If the learned channel routing carries real information about travel time and attenuation, it should raise NSE over the unrouted sum of runoff at most gauges in years it never saw.

For every gauge, we take the model's test NSE minus the summed-Q′ test NSE, then report the typical (median) difference for each group, with how sure we are about that median.
