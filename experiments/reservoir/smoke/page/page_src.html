Dam Release Smoke Test

{{N_DAM}} gauges below a large dam and {{N_CTL}} matched gauges with no dam upstream, across the 18 HUC2 regions. At every one, today's model (no dams) was passed through a small storage-and-release bucket tuned on water years 1983-1995 and scored on 1996-2010. These are the answers the release law in ddrs has to reproduce, and the bar it has to clear.

The set

Who is in it, and why

Dam gauges ({{N_DAM}})

  • Every training gauge with an NID dam of 10 MCM or more upstream, where the nearest such dam drains at least two-thirds of the gauge's area, so the gauge sees that dam's release.
  • That dam was finished by 1980 and snapped to its MERIT reach by drainage-area match. Larger dams further up are allowed: {{N_CASCADE}} gauges sit below a chain of them.
  • At least 80 % of days observed in both WY1982-1995 and WY1996-2010; drainage area at most 25,000 km².
  • {{N_ONREACH}} dams sit on the gauge's own reach. HUC 08 and 09 had no gauge under these rules and each got one with the dam further up (area ratio up to 10), marked relaxed.

Controls ({{N_CTL}})

  • One per dam gauge: no NID dam upstream, no "regulated" flag on its annual peaks, the same coverage rule.
  • Same HUC2 region and the closest drainage area in log terms, each used once, largest dam gauges matched first. {{N_CROSS}} came from another region when a region ran out.
  • They answer one question: does a tuned bucket help because there is a dam, or because it smooths a model that is too flashy everywhere?

What was run

  • The trained no-dam model (sr_n0_gamma, n₀ and γ learned) routed hourly over WY1982-2010 in one pass on all {{N_ALL}} gauges: {{REACHES}} MERIT reaches, {{EVAL_MIN}} minutes on a CPU.
  • At every gauge, two buckets fitted to the observed daily flow on WY1983-1995 (WY1982 warms up the storage), scored on WY1996-2010: a plain one, S = T₀·Q, and a seasonal one whose response time swings over the year, T = T₀·exp(a sin ω + b cos ω).
  • No dam data enters: the only input is the model's own routed flow, and the gauge record is only the target.

Does the bucket help below dams more than elsewhere?

Hypothesis

If the release law captures what dams do, tuning it should raise NSE at most dam gauges and leave the matched controls close to where they were. If it only smooths a flashy model, both groups should move by about the same amount.

In plain words

NSE is 1 for a perfect hydrograph and 0 for no better than the mean flow. It is measured with and without the tuned bucket at every gauge. Each line sorts one group's changes from worst to best; a line further right means bigger gains.

Next

What the implementation must show on this set

  1. Pass-through start. With every dam at T₀ = 1 hour, ddrs reproduces the no-dam run at all {{N_ALL}} gauges to within 1e-4 m³/s.
  2. Engine matches this fit. With the KAN head frozen and each gauge's nearest dam set to the fitted T₀, a and b, ddrs matches these tuned hydrographs at the {{N_ONREACH}} dams on their gauge's reach (NSE between the two above 0.99).
  3. Gradients. T₀, a and b pass a finite-difference check.
  4. Learning. Training on 1981-1995 from the pass-through start moves T₀ off 1 hour where this fit found storage and leaves it near 1 hour where it found none; controls have no dam and keep none.
  5. Beat the controls. {{CHECK5}}

Scripts and results: experiments/reservoir/smoke/ on branch reservoir-options (select_smoke_gauges.py, run_smoke_eval.sh, expected_release_fit.py). Dams: USACE National Inventory of Dams, snapped to MERIT-Basins reaches. Observations: USGS NWIS daily discharge.
Model: ddrs run 2026-09-12T23-39-03Z at epoch 50 (n₀ and γ learned, no leakance, no dams), CPU. Scores are daily NSE and KGE over water years 1996-2010.
Basemap: HUC2 water-resource regions from the USGS 1:2,000,000 Hydrologic Units (National Atlas), as dissolved by the Iowa DNR (huc_02, distributed with the CAMELS basin dataset), projected to CONUS Albers (EPSG:5070) and simplified to about 3.5 km.
