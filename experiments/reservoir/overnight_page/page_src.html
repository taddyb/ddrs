ddrs · learned dam release · overnight 27–28 September 2026 · smoke set, test water years 1996–2010

Per-Dam Release Calibration

The goal was a median NSE gain of +0.03 at gauges where dams matter. Overnight the channel routing was frozen, so only dam parameters train. Each dam got its own residence time and seasonal rule curve, the dam row stopped replacing the reach's channel routing, and the water the discharge floor creates is now measured. The best trained arm gains at the 117 target gauges. Run through the engine, the best offline-fitted per-dam parameters reach about +0.02. The target was not reached.

Setup

What was compared, and on which years

The smoke set has 916 gauges: 458 below an NID dam of at least 10 MCM, each paired with an undammed control in the same HUC2 region and closest in drainage area. The target gauges are the 117 dam gauges whose dam sits on the gauge's own reach and whose degree of regulation (DOR, upstream storage over one year of flow) is above 0.5. Every number below is the per-gauge change in test-year NSE against the same no-dam model at the same gauge, summarised as a median with a 95 % bootstrap interval.

Frozen routing: the channel network (Manning's n and γ per reach) is loaded from the smoke no-dam run and never trained again, so undammed gauges cannot change and every gain belongs to the dams. The jointly trained arm from the earlier session moved its controls by up to 0.345 NSE; the frozen arms move them by exactly 0.

The main result

Every arm against the +0.03 target

Hypothesis

If per-dam parameters are the missing physics and training can find them, trained arms should approach what the same laws reach with parameters fitted offline per dam and replayed through the engine.

Plain English

Trained arms learn dam parameters by gradient descent through the routing. Replays take parameters fitted offline from each gauge's own training years and route them with no training: the in-engine ceiling of each law. The control arms the dams with one hour of storage and nothing else.

Attribution

What made the difference

A review warned that the new cap on the dam row (which keeps its inflow coefficient c1 from going negative) changes routing at dam reaches on most steps, so the replay gains might belong to the cap. Paired controls answer it:

The cap does nothing by itself. It doubles what a per-dam residence time gains, because the channel's negative c1 at low flow fought the reservoir storage and made the discharge floor create water. Gauge by gauge, the rule curve adds about +0.001 on top of the fitted residence time, as the offline audit found.

Water accounting

How much water the discharge floor creates

When a dam row's solution falls below the discharge floor, the post-solve clamp lifts it and forgives the deficit: water appears that no inflow supplied. The first account under-reported this by a factor of (K + T)/dt; the corrected one reads it off the dam's routed volume balance. Per dam, as a share of the dam's test-period inflow:

"Clean gauges" are target gauges whose dam creates under 0.5 % of its inflow. On every arm the clean-gauge gain is at least as large as the full one, so created water is not what produces the gains. The capped arms create the least.

By degree of regulation

By dam purpose

Result. Conclusion. The gain rises with regulation up to DOR 2 and concentrates at flood-control dams, about four times any other purpose. Their operation, storing a flood and releasing it over the following days, is what a per-dam storage law describes. Water-supply dams gain least, and hydropower and irrigation dams, whose error is mostly seasonal shape and diverted volume, gain a few thousandths, as the earlier seasonal-error analysis predicted.

What changed in the code

New machinery on dam-release-v2

Completion years. A dam routes as an ordinary channel until 1 January of its NID completion year. At the 41 full-population gauges whose dams came online in the test years, the dam gain rose from +0.002 to +0.011.

Frozen routing (release_head.freeze_routing). Only the dam parameters train. Controls are bitwise unchanged.

Additive dam row (dam_row: additive). The reservoir's storage adds to the reach's channel storage, so a zero residence time is the channel again.

Non-negative inflow coefficient on dam rows (dam_row_positivity). Caps the channel wedge so c1 stays above zero.

Per-dam parameters. A residence-time multiplier and a four-coefficient seasonal rule curve per dam, trained with row-sparse Adam, a feasibility penalty and continuous seasonal phase.

Water accounting in release_clamp.csv: created, repaid and owed volume per dam, and an optional mass-conserving floor (dam_floor: carry).

Gates. Every commit passed Tier A, including compare_ddr_sandbox ABSOLUTE MATCH (max abs 1.5e-4 m³/s), and the full test suite (968 passed at the last commit).

Reviews

What the reviews caught

The first rule-curve arms let the flux store more water than a dam receives; the clamp created water and removed the gradient that should have stopped it. Fixed with a feasibility penalty and bounded amplitude.

Per-dam parameters used dense Adam, so dams kept moving for about ten steps after each gradient. Fixed with row-sparse Adam.

The created-water account under-reported by (K + T)/dt. Corrected and checked against a real mass balance; an earlier journal entry was corrected, not rewritten.

The mass-conserving floor pumped debt on the additive row, because the channel's c1 went negative at the floor. The dam-row cap removed the pump.

An independent re-implementation confirmed the offline rule-curve gain of +0.049 with no leakage, and showed most of it is the per-dam residence time.

Limits

What this does not show

  • One seed, the 916-gauge smoke set only. No full-population arm was run, because no trained arm cleared the bar.
  • Per-dam parameters exist only for dams with a gauge downstream; ungauged dams get the head's residence time, which alone gained about +0.007.
  • The S5 arms changed three settings at once, so their lower score is not attributed.
  • Controls are matched on region and area, not on irrigation intensity, so part of the dam gap may be withdrawals near dams.
  • The 30 to 120 day error band, flood storage released over weeks, is still the largest unexplained dam error.
  • The carry floor's debt is outside autograd, so training gets no signal about it.
