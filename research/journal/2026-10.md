- offline: laws_v6 harness, nse-batch training loss on WY1983-1995, test WY1996-2010; 214 on-reach dam gauges (T0, rule-curve amplitude), the 69 for the pool; report and figures `experiments/curvature_dams/report.txt`

**Question:** Is each dam parameter (per-dam T0, rule-curve amplitude, pool size z, release target kc, capture share phi) identifiable from the downstream gauge, meaning curved at its optimum, reachable from the engine's init, and stationary from training to test years? Is the pool's flat start in the engine's forward and not only in the offline harness?

**What we did:**

- Per-gauge 1-D and 2-D training-loss landscapes: curvature at the optimum, 0.02 / 0.005 NSE bands, gradient at the engine init vs halfway, multimodality, train-to-test transfer; routing n and q from the landscape census on the same scale.
- Engine replays at four pool sizes against the offline harness at the same z.

**Result:** Pool's own test gain over z = 0.05 d, engine / offline: 1 d +0.0042 / +0.0041, 5 d +0.0145 / +0.0142, 20 d +0.0272 / +0.0267, fitted (median 17 d) +0.0283 / +0.0277; per-gauge Spearman 0.92-0.98; a 0.05-d pool differs from no pool by +0.0002 (max 0.0011). Curvature at the optimum vs routing n (0.047): z 0.045 but plateau-at-init at 72 % (init gradient 7 % of halfway), transfer 1.12; kc 0.54, multimodal at 97 %, plateau at 71 %; phi has a boundary optimum at 1 (67 %) and fixing it costs 0.0000; rule-curve amplitude 0.041 with no plateau but only 19 % of its training gain on test years; T0 flat below T0* 0.5 d (80 %), 0.10 above 2 d where half start on the shelf.

**Conclusion:** Supported: the pool cannot be learned from off because the engine's forward is flat over z 0.05 to 1 d (the pool is full on 97-99 % of flood days, so kc and phi never enter, and z's gradient scales with the volume it moves). Next: initialise pools open (5-20 d) or calibrate z and kc per gauged dam, fix phi = 1, drop or shrink the rule curve, learn T0 per dam without L2 from a 1-2 d init. Curvature alone would pass every dam parameter but phi; reachability and stationarity are what separate them.
