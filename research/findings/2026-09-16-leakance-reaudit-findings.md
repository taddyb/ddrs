# Leakance re-audit: a frozen parameter, an anchored port, and a term that still does not learn (2026-09-16)

Prompted by a request to revisit leakance for bugs and for gradient or process improvements, not to
re-open the identifiability verdict. The verdict is unchanged. What follows is what the audit found,
what was fixed, and what the first runs on the fixed code show.

## 1. The exchange rate was never a learned parameter

`src/routing/utils.rs::denormalize` guarded its log-space branch with `(lo + 1e-6).ln()`. The guard is
meant for a zero lower bound. It swallows any box whose lower bound is small next to one part in a
million.

| box | value at u=0 | value at u=1 | log span, actual | log span, intended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K_D, `[1e-8, 1e-6]` | 1.01e-6 | 1.00e-6 | −0.0100 | +4.6052 |
| K_D, Phase C `[1e-8, 1e-5]` | 1.01e-6 | 1.00e-5 | +2.2926 | +6.9078 |
| p_spatial, `[1, 200]` | 1 | 200 | +5.2983 | +5.2983 |

So `K_D` was a constant near 1e-6 with an inverted map, in every leakance run ever executed, and the
width coefficient was untouched because its lower bound is 1. Three statements in the July record are
consequently wrong: `K_D` "pinned at the ceiling" was the collapsed band read as a training outcome;
its correlation with aridity was computed across a 1 % span; and Phase C's widening was 10x, not 1000x.

The NO-GO verdict is unaffected. The reachable flux set is the same either way, because
`leakance_factor` spans `[0, 1]` and covers the same product; every verdict-bearing measurement was made
on the flux field rather than the parameter triple; and the detectability result does not depend on
parameterisation. Fixed in PR #46, along with the same guard in the inverse
`src/training/forward.rs::physical_to_normalized`, now sharing one helper so the two cannot drift.

The defect is inherited verbatim from DDR's `routing/utils.py::denormalize` and needs the same patch
upstream. Correcting our side moved the DDR sandbox comparison from 1.53e-5 to 1.53e-4 m3/s, still an
absolute match with 6.5x margin, because the fixture was generated by code that still carries the guard.

## 2. The port is now anchored to the reference

Leakance was the one part of the routing core anchored to nothing, because DDR reverted the feature.
Its guards were all self-consistency and none would catch a misread formula. DDR commit `c2bd0f9`
still carries `_compute_zeta`, so a fixture was generated from it and compared (PR #47).

    leakance tail parity (reference depth):     max rel diff 6.169857e-6, 29 reaches, REFERENCE MATCH
    leakance full-chain parity (ddrs depth):    max rel diff 4.567296e-6, 28 of 29 reaches

The tolerance is derived, not tuned. The `q_eps = q_spatial + 1e-6` width exponent perturbs the flux by
a relative `eps * ln(p*d)`, which predicts 6.2e-6 on this fixture against 6.169857e-6 measured. That one
term accounts for the entire difference; everything else sits at the f32 floor.

## 3. Two deliberate divergences from the reference, both worth knowing

**The losing-only clamp defaults to on.** The reference is sign-symmetric and its own docstring says a
negative flux means a gaining stream. Ours clamps at zero by default, so leakance can only remove water.
The entire July campaign therefore tested a one-sided term and gaining reaches were never reachable.

**The depth floor bites at low flow.** The reference applies no lower bound to depth inside the flux
computation; ours shares the routing core's `attribute_minimums.depth`. The one sub-floor reach in the
fixture carries **3.406x** the reference flux, exactly `(d_lb/d_ref)^(1+q)`. This is the largest of the
three deviations and it acts precisely in the regime where a losing stream is most plausible.

## 4. Juniata, on the fixed code: the fix works and the term still does not learn

Matched pair on the committed bundle, `examples/juniata/ddrs.yaml` against the new
`examples/juniata/ddrs_leakance.yaml`, differing only by the three leakance outputs and `use_leakance`.

| arm | median NSE | median KGE |
|---|---|---|
| control, leakance off | 0.7903 | 0.8810 |
| leakance on | 0.7914 | 0.8807 |

The control reproduces the published bundle benchmark (0.790 / 0.881) exactly, so the fix leaves the
leakance-off path unchanged. The leakance arm's gain sits inside the documented four-seed spread
(0.790 to 0.800), so it is noise.

The parameters are the result. After 30 epochs all three leakance outputs sit essentially at
initialisation, while the channel parameters in the same run moved:

| parameter | learned range | box | fraction of box used |
|---|---|---|---|
| K_D | 9.798e-8 to 1.117e-7 | 1e-8 to 1e-6 | 0.057 of 2 decades |
| d_gw | 0.0036 to 0.1447 | −2 to 2 | under 4 % |
| leakance_factor | 0.490 to 0.513 | 0 to 1 | under 3 % |
| n (for contrast) | 0.110 to 0.131 | 0.015 to 0.25 | moved off centre |
| p_spatial (for contrast) | 10.5 to 14.3 | 1 to 200 | moved off centre |

`K_D` now sits near 1e-7, the log-space centre of its box, with none at the ceiling, which is what the
fix was supposed to deliver. It simply does not move from there. The learned flux has median
5.8e-4 m3/s against the July go-ahead bar of 0.01, and `zeta` equals `zeta_net` at every reach, which
confirms the clamp is active and nothing is gaining.

Caveats: one basin, one gauge, 30 epochs, and the L1 objective rather than the batch-NSE recipe the
CONUS arms use. This is a sanity check, not the test.

## 5. CONUS, against the retrospective benchmark

`config/experiments/sr_n0_leakance.yaml`, a single-variable change against `sr_n0_only.yaml`: same
gauges, windows, loss and seed, on the UH retrospective store.

**The 500-update arm diverged.** Loss and the head's own outputs went NaN at epoch 15, mini-batch 8,
and the remaining 36 epochs trained on NaN weights. The failure is in the backward, not the forward:
mini-batch 7 reported a finite loss (0.188355) and the checkpoint written after its optimizer step is
already NaN, so a non-finite gradient was produced from a finite forward and applied. Cutting the peak
learning rate 5x removed it, so this is a step-size problem rather than a structural defect. The July
campaign never met it because a frozen K_D was acting as a stabiliser.

Both arms below were then rerun at the lowered rate (peak 0.001, 20 epochs), matched to each other.
They are NOT comparable to the 0.7376 / 0.7600 reference, which uses the full 500-update recipe.

| arm | median NSE | median KGE |
|---|---|---|
| leakance off, matched control | 0.7345 | 0.7596 |
| leakance on | 0.7382 | 0.7627 |
| difference | +0.0037 | +0.0031 |

**The volume-absorption hypothesis is refuted.** The retrospective product over-delivers volume by
about 12 % at the median. The learned loss removes this:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| median zeta | 4.268e-4 m3/s |
| zeta / mean discharge, median | 0.0441 % |
| zeta / mean discharge, p90 | 0.1743 % |
| reaches clearing the 0.01 m3/s bar | 11.6 % of 64,892 |
| gaining reaches (`zeta_net` < 0) | 0 |

Two orders of magnitude short of the bias it would have to absorb, with parameters chosen freely from
boxes they now span. The +0.004 skill difference is small enough that it is at least as likely to come
from three extra head outputs as from the water removed, and no seed replicate exists at this recipe
to separate them. Do not quote it as a leakance benefit.

Two mechanisms are visible in the learned fields. Zero gaining reaches, because the losing-only clamp
defaults on. And the learned groundwater offset (median +0.43 m) sits **above** the median channel
depth (0.26 m), so under that clamp the model is using the offset as an off switch at most reaches
rather than as a water-table elevation. Whether an unclamped, sign-symmetric term behaves differently
is untested and is the one variant worth a further run.

This completes the paper's sentence in the other direction. Roughness absorbs the inflow product's
timing. Leakance does not absorb its volume, because at physically admissible conductances the term is
far too small to matter.

- `src/experiment/landscape/objective.rs` hard-codes `k_d` and `leakance_factor` to `None`, so a
  landscape probe of a leakance arm silently measures the model without leakance. Not yet biting,
  because no landscape study has been run on such an arm.
- `tests/cuda_backward_parity.rs` has no leakance coverage. Deliberately deferred; adding an arm needs
  non-vacuity guards of its own or it reproduces the failure mode recorded in that file's header.
