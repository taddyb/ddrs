### 2026-09-12 · head topology, and stage-dependent roughness <!-- id:session-head-topology-stage-roughness-2026-09-12 -->

**Question:** Two, pursued in one session. (1) Is the KAN head the reason the learned parameters
collapse onto one another, and would a separate KAN for `p` and `q` fix it? (2) Can Manning's
roughness be made to depend on stage, and does that help?

**What we did:**

- Built `src/bin/head_arch_screen.rs`, which compares head topologies over the real CONUS attribute
  distribution with no routing and no training, plus an 8-seed sweep because a single initialisation
  cannot rank them (chance `|rho|` is `1/sqrt(effective rank)`, which is 0.479 here).
- Measured the trained trunk directly for the first time, via a new `--checkpoint` mode, and walked
  it across 12 checkpoints of the 500-update run.
- Trained `head_shared_linear` (`2026-09-11T23-24-04Z-train-and-test`): the 500-update `nse-batch`
  baseline with `p_spatial` made learnable alongside `n` and `q_spatial`, nothing else changed. Three
  further arms (split trunk, wider, KAN read-out) were queued and then cancelled at the user's
  request to pursue stage roughness instead.
- Implemented stage-dependent roughness `n(d) = n_0·(d/d_ref)^(−gamma)` as a fixed forward-model
  parameter (Phase 1 of its design doc; `gamma` is NOT learned and does not reach the KAN).
- Screened `gamma` on Juniata (~20 s per arm) as a 2x2 against `q`'s range, then as a 7-point sweep.
- Launched a matched CONUS pair, `gamma` 0 and 0.35.

**Result:**

- **The head is capable, and §31's architectural verdict was wrong.** All seven topologies, the
  current one included, decorrelate two supervised targets to affine R² = 0.0000. The attributes
  carry effective rank 6.11 of 10, and the trunk 4.36 of 21 at initialisation, so neither is
  one-dimensional.
- **Training is what collapses it.** The trunk falls from rank 4.36 to **1.38** (PC1 0.41 -> 0.85),
  and the trajectory shows half the collapse done inside epoch 1 and nothing changing over the
  remaining 48. Depth makes it worse (rank 3.41 at 4 layers), width better (6.41 at H=64).
- **`p_spatial` learnable is worth +0.008 NSE**: 0.7458 / 0.7619 against 0.7376 / 0.7600 on the same
  2,365 gauges. One seed, and on a binary that is master plus the `kan_head` restructure.
- **`gamma` helps Juniata a lot and not for the predicted reason.** The sweep is smooth and
  single-peaked: 0.7903 at `gamma = 0` rising to **0.8813 / 0.9344 at `gamma = 0.35`**, then falling
  at 0.50. The control reproduces the documented 0.7903 / 0.8810 exactly. Narrowing `q` toward the
  Leopold & Maddock at-a-station value HURT (−0.025). At the trained `q ≈ 0.084` the model already
  sits at a velocity exponent of 0.381 against an observed 0.34, so `gamma` moves the static
  exponents further from observation while improving skill.
- `n(d)` swings **2.31x** across the water year on Juniata, and its time series is the hydrograph
  upside down.

**Conclusion:** The architecture was never the wall; the gradient is. That reframes §31 from "the
KAN is defective" to "a capable network faithfully representing a nearly one-dimensional objective",
which is the paper's own selective-equifinality thesis showing up in the weights. `gamma` is the
first parameter added after checking it acts on the identifiable channel rather than before, and its
smooth interior optimum is what an identifiable parameter looks like — the opposite of `q`, whose
Hessian is a saddle at half of gauges. But the Juniata optimum sits above the literature value, so
`gamma` is probably also absorbing the model's too-deep flood geometry, and that is a caveat carried

was built to answer was really about the other three arms; the `p` result is what it delivered
before the chain was cancelled.

**What we did:**

- Took the 500-update `nse-batch` baseline `2026-09-10T21-21-48Z-conus-train-and-test`
  (NSE 0.7376 / KGE 0.7600) and changed only the `kan_head` block, so the single manipulated
  variable is that `p_spatial` moves from fixed at 21 to learned.
- Same 2,365 gauges, same eval window, same optimizer budget, same seed, same data sources.
- Held against that baseline, not against the summed-Q' bar.

**Result:** median NSE **0.7458** and KGE **0.7619**, against 0.7376 / 0.7600: **+0.0082 NSE,

+0.059 the optimizer-budget fix delivered, and it is a single seed. NdArray is deterministic, so
re-running reproduces it exactly and gives no spread estimate.

**Conclusion:** The skill gain is real; **the mechanism predicted for it is refuted** (findings
§33.1). §32.5 argued that with `p` constant the downstream width exponent is capped at `q·f`, and
that `p` is the only parameter able to supply the rest through how it scales with river size.
Measured: `p` scales *negatively* with discharge (`beta = −0.144`), so although `q` more than
tripled (0.084 → 0.295, lifting `q·f` from 0.046 to 0.163), the net downstream width exponent FELL
from 0.099 to **0.004** against Leopold & Maddock's 0.50. Channel width is now essentially constant
from headwater to main stem. **Skill improved and the channel geometry got worse.**

carries rank 1.64 rather than 1.38, and the share of `q`'s pre-activation explained by `n`'s falls
from 99.2 % to 45.8 %, with the three fields separating onto different attributes for the first time
(`q` becomes slope-driven at rho = +0.665).

This is selective equifinality caught in the act, and it is more interesting than the +0.008: given a
free parameter the model spent it on whatever helped the daily hydrograph and none of it on making
channels defensible, because the objective cannot see channel width (§30). The operational lesson is
that **`b` must be reported alongside NSE for every future arm** — skill alone would have logged this
as a clean improvement.

One caveat remains: a single seed on a deterministic backend, so there is no spread estimate. The
binary question is closed — a plain-master build (`3412a78`) and this branch's build produce
identical per-micro-batch losses on this config to six decimals, so the `kan_head` restructure and
the stage-roughness code are both numerically inert at their defaults and the +0.008 belongs to
`p_spatial`. The other three arms (split trunk, wider, KAN read-out) were cancelled in favour of
stage-dependent roughness and never ran.
